# West Peek Ventures Community — Operating Scaffolding

**This is the source of truth for the community model.** Events OS and Community OS are built
against it. Where the code and this document disagree, this document is right and the code is a bug.

Recorded 17 Aug 2026 from the operator. The section at the end, *How this document became code*,
is the only part written by engineering.

## What This Is

The West Peek Ventures Community is a free professional mastermind network for ambitious operators,
founders, investors, and specialists.

It is not a networking group, a course, or a founder accelerator.

It is a relationship ecosystem designed around one simple belief:

> Good people should meet good people.

Our goal is to create an environment where thoughtful, ambitious people repeatedly help one another
solve real problems. As trust compounds, West Peek naturally becomes part of the conversations where
companies, partnerships, and liquidity opportunities begin.

The output is not engagement.

**The output is early inclusion.**

## The Core Thesis

Most investors meet founders when they're raising capital.

We want to meet them while they're still:

- thinking about leaving their job
- validating an idea
- looking for a cofounder
- making their first hires
- considering a secondary sale
- helping another founder solve a problem

Those conversations happen between operators, lawyers, recruiters, executives, and founders — not
investors.

The community simply puts West Peek inside those relationships.

## The Three-Layer Model

### 1. The Community (Open Mastermind)

This is the heart of everything. Membership is free.

Rather than existing as another Slack or Discord, the community functions as a continuous
professional mastermind that members can tap into whenever they're building something, facing a
challenge, or looking to help others.

Members come to ask thoughtful questions, solve real business problems, find collaborators, hire
people, make introductions, share what they're building, and help other members succeed.

Most members will spend their entire journey here. **That's intentional.** The community itself
should provide tremendous value.

#### The Monthly Mastermind Rhythm

Every month the entire community gets two anchor experiences.

**Community Mastermind** — a 75–90 minute virtual mastermind open to every member.

Structure: community updates → live member problem submissions → small breakout mastermind groups
(6–8 people) → introductions and commitments.

Members should leave every session with better decisions, new relationships, and actionable next
steps. This becomes the heartbeat of the community.

**Monthly Room** — each month West Peek hosts one focused Room around a specific theme. Examples:
Zero-to-One, Hiring Your First Team, Healthcare Innovation, Consumer Brands, AI Builders, Capital
Strategy, Founder Mental Models, Scaling Operations.

Some Rooms are virtual, hybrid, in-person, sponsored, application-based, or invite-only.

The Room format gives the larger mastermind intimacy without forcing members into permanent cohorts.

### 2. West Peek Rooms

Rooms are curated experiences — pop-up environments built around one important topic, industry, or
problem: dinners, workshops, salons, deep-work sessions, operator roundtables, excursions, regional
gatherings.

Rooms become the primary way members build deeper relationships. They are also **the primary
monetization layer.**

Sponsors support the experience — not access to members.

West Peek owns the guest list, programming, facilitation, and community. Sponsors simply help make
better experiences possible.

### 3. The Council

The Council is the earned inner network.

It is not an application. It is not based on status. It emerges naturally from consistent
participation.

Members enter because they repeatedly demonstrate generosity, good judgment, execution,
follow-through, thoughtful leadership, and trustworthiness.

The Council becomes West Peek's extended partnership network. Members receive rotating small-group
conversations, private dinners, curated workshops, deeper relationships, opportunities to host
Rooms, and occasional diligence conversations.

This is where trust compounds the fastest.

## Natural Progression

There is no formal promotion process. The path simply becomes:

Join the Community → Participate in Masterminds → Attend Rooms → Build relationships → Become
someone others trust → Join the Council

**Not everyone needs to move inward.** Most members will remain valuable contributors inside the
broader mastermind forever.

## Operating Rhythm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** | The Office — virtual coworking. Members quietly work together. Creates familiarity and casual interaction. Runs on westpeek.live as *Tap In Tuesday* / *Deep Work Wednesday*. |
| **Monthly** | Community Mastermind · Monthly Room |
| **Quarterly** | Regional hybrid gatherings · Curated dinners · Workshops |
| **Annually** | Large West Peek Community Summit · Special Council experiences |

Conversation — not presentations — is the product.

## West Peek's Role

We are not influencers. We are not event organizers. We are not constantly teaching.

We are **curators and conveners.** Our job is to make thoughtful introductions, notice talented
people, create meaningful Rooms, facilitate useful conversations, and protect the quality of the
community.

We stay visible without becoming the center of attention.

## Business Model

Membership remains free. Revenue comes from sponsored Rooms, ecosystem partnerships, annual summit
sponsors, hospitality partners, and carefully selected strategic partners.

**Sponsors support experiences. They never purchase access to members.**

## Why This Matters

The community creates reach. The monthly mastermind creates habit. Rooms create deeper
relationships. The Council creates trust.

Together they create a professional ecosystem where ambitious people consistently help one
another — and where West Peek naturally becomes part of important decisions before they become
public opportunities.

Instead of chasing deals, we become part of the environment where deals, companies, and careers are
first imagined.

> Community creates reach.
> Masterminds create momentum.
> Rooms create connection.
> The Council creates trust.

# The first monetized Room

## The Zero-to-One Room

*"What actually changes when an operator becomes a founder?"*

A curated working dinner or hybrid salon for 25–35 strong operators, first-time founders, startup
lawyers, finance leaders, and technical builders.

Participants submit real questions anonymously beforehand:

- When is an idea worth leaving your job for?
- How do you test demand without publicly launching?
- How should you choose a cofounder?
- What legal, equity, cloud, and finance infrastructure matters on day one?
- How much runway do you need before fundraising?

Keep presentations to 10 minutes maximum. Most of the evening should be facilitated tables solving
actual situations.

### Why this should go first

It directly advances West Peek's fund strategy: you meet people during company formation rather
than once their pitch deck is circulating.

It is also easy for sponsors to understand, because nearly every new founder must choose cloud
infrastructure, banking and spend tools, cap-table/equity software, legal counsel, and payroll and
hiring systems. That gives natural sponsors without making members feel like inventory.

### Sponsors to approach first

**1. AWS Startups — best lead sponsor.** *"Own the moment between idea and first product."* AWS is
actively building relationships with startups from pre-seed onward through AWS Activate. Ask for
$15,000–$25,000 presenting sponsorship, AWS credits for eligible participants, one startup
specialist in the room, optional technical office hours afterward.

**2. Ramp — strongest fintech prospect.** *"Help new founders build their company's operating system
correctly from day one."* Ramp runs founder and finance-leader events, including small curated
gatherings. Ask for $10,000–$20,000, dinner or venue underwriting, startup offers for attendees, one
finance operator — not a salesperson.

**3. Carta — strongest equity sponsor.** *"Be present when founders make their first consequential
ownership decisions."* Ask for $10,000–$15,000, an equity setup clinic after the Room, Carta Launch
benefits for qualified founders, anonymized market benchmarks for the discussion.

**4. Cooley, Orrick, Gunderson or Wilson Sonsini.** *"Build trusted relationships with future
founders before they formally need counsel."* Choose **one** legal sponsor so the event does not
feel crowded with firms. Ask for $10,000–$20,000, a lawyer participating as a problem solver, a
limited number of follow-up founder consultations, **no attendee-list harvesting.**

**5. Brex.** *"Support ambitious operators at the point where they become company builders."* A
credible alternate to Ramp, but not in the same Room — they occupy competing categories.

### Recommended sponsor structure

Do not start with six logos.

- One presenting partner: $20,000–$25,000
- One supporting partner: $7,500–$12,500
- One in-kind partner: venue, food, travel or member benefits

Target total revenue for the pilot: **$30,000–$45,000.**

Preferred first combination: AWS Startups presents The Zero-to-One Room, supporting partner Carta or
one startup law firm. AWS owns building, Carta/legal owns formation, and West Peek owns the
relationships.

The sponsor pitch is *"you are underwriting a trusted founder-formation experience,"* not *"buying
access to 5,000 members."* That distinction protects the community while making the Room valuable
enough to sponsor.

# How this document became code

Written by engineering. Seven decisions were taken reading the model above, each because a
reasonable engineer would otherwise have built the opposite.

### 1. "Sponsors never purchase access" is enforced, not documented

`src/shared/community/sponsorPolicy.ts`. Sponsor representatives *do* attend — the AWS specialist,
the Ramp finance operator, the lawyer solving problems. The rule is not "no contact with members";
it is **no member data leaves West Peek.** No attendee export, no post-event contact file, no
follow-up list. Enforced as a function rather than a sentence, so it still holds under pressure to
close a deal.

### 2. There is no member stage field, and no score

The document says most members stay in the broad mastermind forever and that this is intentional. A
`stage` column would make every such member look stalled and would produce a "conversion rate to
Council" — the exact status hierarchy the Council is not.

Instead `com_act` records **observable acts**. Council is a flag a human sets with that evidence in
view. Nothing is ranked. See `src/shared/community/acts.ts`, which will fail its own tests if a
scoring function is added.

### 3. Only acts West Peek witnessed are recorded

A member promising another member an introduction is between them (operator, 17 Aug 2026). The OS
does not watch interactions West Peek is not part of. "Follow-through" therefore means follow-through
on commitments made **to West Peek** — agreed to host, agreed to speak, agreed to bring three
people.

### 4. Early inclusion is measured as deal provenance, not engagement

The document rules out engagement metrics by name. The measurement that matches "early inclusion" is
where a deal's relationship started and how long before the deal existed. Captured on the
opportunity at creation, because nobody remembers it later.

If no deal traces back to a Room after eighteen months, the model is not working and that should be
visible.

### 5. Every venue contact in a Room packet carries a source and is unverified until called

An AI asked for a venue's phone number will produce a confident, wrong one. Same discipline as
market mapping: no source URL, no entry — and what survives is labelled unverified pending a call.
A packet whose first phone number is dead is a packet nobody opens again.

### 5b. Anything known about a person expires

Matching needed a way to say "Ada is looking for a job" by hand, because the Mastermind only
produces signal in the months it runs (operator, 17 Aug 2026). `rel_signal` is that, and every row
has a NOT NULL `expires_at` defaulting to about four months.

The expiry is the safety feature, not housekeeping. An introduction proposed on a job hunt that
ended a year ago is embarrassing in front of the person it is about, so a forgotten note goes quiet
rather than firing forever. Signals are confidential by default: that someone is job-hunting while
employed is exactly the thing that must not leak.

### 6. Matching proposes; it never sends

Introductions are tuned for precision over volume, because an intro spends West Peek's reputation
and a weekly stream of mediocre suggestions kills the feature and takes credibility with it.
Complementarity — need meeting experience — beats profile similarity, and the signal comes from
mastermind questions and answers rather than a profile form that rots.

### 7. Members are records, not accounts

Where the continuous community eventually lives is undecided (operator, 17 Aug 2026 — possibly a
white-labelled platform). So there is no member auth and no second identity table; membership
attaches to `person`. Every act carries a `source`, so a future platform is an adapter rather than a
rewrite.

Consequence worth stating plainly: **until that surface exists the community is event-based, not
continuous.** The Mastermind, the Rooms and the weekly Office all work without it. "Tap in on a
Tuesday with a problem" is, for now, Tap In Tuesday on westpeek.live.
